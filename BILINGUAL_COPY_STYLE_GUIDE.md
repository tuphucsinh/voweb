# VOrigin Bilingual Copy Style Guide

Version: 1.0 — aligned with website rc5
Languages: Vietnamese (VI) and International/British English (EN)
Brand promise: **From Origins to Value**

## 1. Voice in one line

**Quiet confidence, clear standards, human warmth.**

VOrigin should sound like a selective, dependable trade partner with taste — never like an advertising template, a logistics brochure, or an AI-generated corporate website.

## 2. What “premium” means in VOrigin copy

Premium is expressed through restraint, precision and rhythm. It is **not** expressed by adding luxury adjectives.

Prefer:
- clear, concrete language
- short confident statements
- specific standards and actions
- calm emotional language
- generous whitespace around important phrases

Avoid:
- inflated claims
- too many adjectives
- hype, urgency or hard selling
- buzzwords and consultant language
- vague promises that cannot be evidenced

## 3. Voice pillars

### Trusted
Say what VOrigin does, what it looks for, and what is verified. Where information is not approved, say so plainly.

### Refined
Use fewer, better words. Let the visual design carry some of the emotion.

### Human
Write as a thoughtful business person would speak to a serious partner. Vary sentence length naturally. Do not force slogans into every paragraph.

### Long-term
Favour language around standards, fit, relationships, market relevance and durable value over short-term volume.

### International
English should read as original English, not as a literal translation of Vietnamese. Vietnamese should read naturally to a Vietnamese business audience, not like translated corporate copy.

## 4. English standard

Use **International/British English** for editorial copy:
- flavours
- localisation
- labelling
- enquiry

Use established proper names and technical terms as officially styled.

Preferred tone examples:
- “Lasting value begins with a trusted origin.”
- “Clear information. Clear commitments.”
- “The aim is to build a lasting market presence together, not simply move a shipment.”

Avoid awkward constructions such as:
- “We bring together comprehensive solutions…”
- “Our ecosystem empowers brands…”
- “Unlock the potential of the Vietnamese market…”
- “Elevate your brand to the next level…”

## 5. Vietnamese standard

Use polished modern Vietnamese: respectful, direct and natural.

Preferred:
- nguồn gốc đáng tin
- cách làm chỉn chu
- tiêu chuẩn rõ ràng
- phù hợp với thị trường
- giá trị bền lâu / giá trị dài hạn
- cùng phát triển
- bắt đầu từ một cuộc trao đổi rõ ràng

Avoid:
- excessive Sino-Vietnamese formality
- ornate luxury language
- literal translations of English business idioms
- claims such as “hàng đầu”, “đẳng cấp”, “tối ưu”, “toàn diện” unless objectively supported

## 6. Words and patterns to avoid

Do not use these as default marketing filler:
- world-class
- seamless
- unlock
- elevate
- transformative
- cutting-edge
- innovative (unless describing a verifiable innovation)
- ecosystem
- empower
- holistic
- bespoke
- next-level
- game-changing
- leverage
- synergy
- redefine / reimagine

Use **curated / tuyển chọn / chọn lọc** only where it carries real brand meaning; do not repeat it in every section.

Avoid repetitive AI-style structures:
- “It’s not just X — it’s Y.”
- “More than just…” repeatedly
- “Whether you’re X, Y, or Z…” as a default opening
- repeated three-adjective sequences
- repeated em-dash clauses
- slogan-like sentences in every paragraph

## 8. Product-claim rules

Product copy must be evidence-led.

AI, editors and CMS users must **not infer or invent**:
- health benefits
- nutrition benefits
- “no preservatives” or similar formulation claims
- certifications
- manufacturing origin
- exclusive distribution rights
- partnership status
- dates/history
- Vietnam-market label claims

For MARIGOLD, flavour range and reference pack information may use the approved official source. Vietnam-market statements must follow VOrigin-approved labels and import documentation.

## 9. Translation rule

Never translate sentence-by-sentence just to preserve structure.

Workflow:
1. Preserve the fact and intent.
2. Rewrite naturally for the target audience.
3. Match the emotional temperature, not the word order.
4. Recheck business/legal meaning.
5. Remove anything that sounds like generic advertising.

## 10. Final editorial test

Before publishing, ask:
- Does this sound like a real person at VOrigin?
- Is every factual claim supportable?
- Is the sentence doing useful work?
- Could 20 other corporate websites say the same thing unchanged?
- Can one adjective be removed without losing meaning?
- Does EN read naturally to an international partner?
- Does VI feel written in Vietnamese rather than translated into Vietnamese?

If the answer is no, rewrite before publishing.
